# Infant vision simulator: primary-source audit

The evidence supports age-related changes in grating acuity, contrast detection, color discrimination and accommodation. It does **not** establish that a camera filter reproduces an infant’s visual experience. Below, measured thresholds, values derived from a published fit, and rendering choices are kept separate. This audit changes no application code.

## Acuity: Brown (2018) and Neijzen (2025)

**Newborn measurement.** [Brown, Opoku & Stenger, *Neonatal Contrast Sensitivity and Visual Acuity: Basic Psychophysics*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6016435/) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1167/tvst.7.3.18)) measured in-hospital newborns with a fixation-and-refixation card procedure. In the main descending method-of-limits (dLIM) experiment, median grating acuity was **0.783 cycles/degree** (n=22). This is about **20/766 as a grating Snellen equivalent**, not an optotype score. The paper prints 1.558 logMAR alongside these values, but `log10(30 / 0.783) = 1.583`, the value consistent with 20/766. The separate method-of-constant-stimuli (MCS) pilot yielded **1.204 c/deg** (n=47; ≈20/498); do not pool the medians or treat the main value as an exact at-birth norm. Brown's ~30° newborn horizontal field statement is cited background, not a measurement of the cohort.

**Published reference fit.** [Neijzen et al., Appendix A, Table A1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12069961/#aos17447-tbl-0002) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/aos.17447), [machine-readable article](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12069961/fullTextXML)) publishes separate **binocular and monocular 95% prediction limits** in c/deg. This is a meta-analysis and the appendix is the primary source for *its fitted reference data*. The limits are symmetric about the fitted mean on the log scale. For each table age, recover the center (to published rounding) with `sqrt(lower * upper)`; then interpolate `log(center)` against `log(age)` between the bracketing rows to reproduce the code's monthly anchors. This is interpolation of tabulated predictions, not refitting the authors’ spline, and prediction limits describe individual variability, not uncertainty around the estimated center.

All seven binocular values round to the existing [model anchors](../../src/lib/baby-vision/model.ts#L35-L51). Keep the label **binocular behavioral grating acuity** for these values. The Brown newborn card protocol is not TAC II; the join and weekly readout are modeled, not measured weekly norms. The app interpolates **log acuity against linear weeks**, distinct from the log-age interpolation used to reconstruct the monthly anchors above. [Mayer et al. 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7890497/) measured monocular TAC acuity and contributes to Neijzen’s monocular fit: not independent validation of that fit, and not a like-for-like binocular check. [Leone et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1111/aos.12366) was excluded from Neijzen for not using a stage; any agreement is a cross-protocol comparison, not validation of every month. MIMo’s [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09805) cites monocular Neijzen data; do not conflate its rendering curve with this binocular one. A grating cutoff also does not establish recognition acuity, a Gaussian shape, or the filter’s chosen 13.5% transmission there.

## Contrast: behavioral threshold is not VEP threshold or perceived contrast

- [Brown et al. 2018, Methods and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6016435/): median dLIM square-wave grating **Michelson contrast threshold 0.458 at 0.083 c/deg** (n=22; 0.46 is rounded) and **0.330 at 0.303 c/deg** (n=21); MCS pilot **0.330 at 0.083 c/deg** (n=38). These are thresholds for particular targets. The estimated dLIM CSF peak (threshold 0.311 at 0.218 c/deg) is inferred, not a measured threshold valid for arbitrary images.
- [Norcia, Tyler & Hamer 1990, original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2247957/) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0042-6989(90)90028-j)): sweep VEP in **48 infants aged 2–40 weeks and 10 adults**; sine gratings reversed at 12 alternations/s, mean luminance 220 cd/m², threshold extrapolated to zero VEP amplitude. Low-frequency thresholds went from **7% at 2–3 weeks** to a **0.5% asymptote around 9 weeks**, and stayed approximately constant thereafter while sensitivity at higher frequencies improved. **0.22–0.32% was the adult comparison**, not an infant measurement at 13 weeks. The same study's ∼5 c/deg in the first month and 16.3 c/deg at eight months are VEP acuity estimates, not interchangeable with behavioral card acuity.
- The [contrast curve](../../src/lib/baby-vision/model.ts#L53-L65) crosses methods, stimuli, spatial frequencies and age groups. The arithmetic behind the [post’s “150×”](../../src/content/posts/baby-vision.mdx#L19) is about 153 (`0.458 / 0.003`); it is a mixed-study ratio using an adult VEP reference, not an estimate of contrast required “to see anything at all.” The 13-week adult endpoint, interpolation, and square-root conversion of threshold ratio to a global suprathreshold gain are design choices. Neither paper calibrates a pixel gain or establishes that the acuity blur accounts for all residual high-frequency contrast immaturity.

Suggested copy: “In one newborn looking test, the median contrast needed to detect a very coarse grating was about 46%. Sensitivity improved rapidly in subsequent infant studies, although the studies used different tests.”

## Color: success on a task is not a saturation or opponent-channel gain

[Adams, Courage & Mercer 1994, original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7941376/) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0042-6989(94)90127-9)) tested **36 newborns and 34 one-month-olds** with systematically varied achromatic-background luminance. **74%** of newborns discriminated a large **10.5° × 17.5° broadband red** patch across all tested relative luminances; **14% blue, 36% green, 25% yellow** did so in the analogous conditions. Most failures were near the photopic luminance match. One-month-olds improved somewhat but clear discrimination was still observed only for the red patch. This rules out a blanket absence of neonatal color discrimination; it does not say that all other colors look gray, that red appears faint, or that only one hue can ever be detected.

[Varner et al. 1985, original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4024480/) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0042-6989(85)90190-7)) used preferential looking for a specific tritan discrimination (4° 416 nm test, 547 nm surround): most of the youngest one-month-olds failed; most two-month-olds and the other one-month-olds succeeded. It supports early short-wavelength function in that task, not an adult-mature L−M mechanism at week 8. [Skelton et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/cdep.12447) is a review, not a primary gain-calibration study. Evidence consistent with functional trichromacy by roughly three months does not mean adult-like chromatic sensitivity or predict an individual child's timeline. All numerical opponent/saturation [gains](../../src/lib/baby-vision/model.ts#L67-L102) are illustrative settings, not estimates of subjective color, detection probability, or chromatic thresholds.

Suggested copy: “Newborn color discrimination is limited, but not absent. In one study, a majority could distinguish a large red patch from gray across the tested luminances.”

## Optics: geometric blur is not perceptual blur

[Green, Powers & Banks 1980, pp. 829–832 and Appendix](https://doi.org/10.1016/0042-6989(80)90063-2) ([archived original PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/2013id_/http://deepblue.lib.umich.edu:80/bitstream/2027.42/23426/1/0000374.pdf)) gives `ΔD = 17.45 φ_deg / p_mm` (Eq. 8). Inverting gives angular **diameter** `φ_arcmin ≈ 3.44 × |ΔD| × p_mm`; this is geometric, paraxial optics and Eq. 8 already uses refractive index 4/3. Do not apply an extra 0.75 correction to this result. Low acuity can make optical defocus harder to detect. For 1.5 c/deg and 4.2 mm, the paper *states* ±1.4 D, attributing it to Eq. 9; the printed Eq. 9 (`7.03/(p_mm × acuity_cpd)`, ≈0.8 stripe width) actually gives **±1.12 D**. ±1.4 D matches Eq. 8 using one full stripe width. Neither number is a direct infant blur-detection measurement. Green cites unchanged mean acuity of 1.5 c/deg over 30–150 cm in [Salapatek, Bechtold & Bushnell 1976](https://doi.org/10.2307/1128208) as an illustration, not evidence that one focusing plane is the sole usable viewing distance.

Green reports [Banks’s two experiments](https://doi.org/10.2307/1129450) at 25/50/100 cm (4/2/1 D): response slopes **0.41/0.51, 0.75/0.79, 0.78/0.83** for ages 1/2/3 months and **0.94/0.95** for adults. Pupils averaged **4.2, 4.6, 4.6 and 5.2 mm**, respectively, under the specific 8 cd/m² stimulus. The slopes do not determine regression intercept or resting focus; Green explicitly notes the latter wasn't known for infants. [Haynes, White & Held 1965, original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14263778/) reports **median** 19 cm (≈5.26 D) in alert newborns using dynamic retinoscopy and flexible accommodation approximating adult performance by month 4 under its test. That median is not a universal fixed focus or best viewing distance. Do not interpret a 26-week unity response as measured, or add refractive error on top of an observed focus endpoint. Age-only pupil interpolation also ignores luminance and mixes protocols.

In [render.ts](../../src/lib/baby-vision/render.ts#L17-L24), the conversion to an angular blur-circle diameter is consistent with Green. The conversion from a uniform disk to a Gaussian is a separate heuristic: for an ideal circular disk of angular diameter θ, `MTF(f) = 2 J₁(π θ f)/(π θ f)` has its first zero at about `1.22/θ`, not `1/θ`; a Gaussian has no first zero. Adding variances is exact only for composing Gaussian kernels, not proof that independent optical and neural terms are identified by the behavioral measurements (which already include optics under the test conditions). The [fixed-plane blend](../../src/lib/baby-vision/model.ts#L247-L257), assumed fixation distance, relative-depth calibration, and display pixel scale are simulation assumptions, not established by Green’s agreement with Banks’s slopes.

## Evidence boundary for the simulator

Call it an **illustrative rendering informed by infant vision studies**, not a validated reproduction or clinical assessment. Mark each number as measured, derived from published reference bounds, or chosen for rendering. Do not imply that a source citation validates the mapping from a lab response to natural scenes, the interpolated week-by-week trajectory, or what a particular baby sees. Brown, Neijzen, and Green were checked against their full articles; the Norcia, Adams, Varner, and Haynes claims above are limited to what their original abstracts establish.
