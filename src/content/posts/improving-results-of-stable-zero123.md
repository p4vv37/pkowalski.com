<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How does it work?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In short, the method is based on optimizing a neural field (NeRF)  with randomly sampled viewpoints generated by <strong>Stable Zero123</strong> network. This NeRF can be later converted to a textured mesh.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Zero-1-to-3</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://zero123.cs.columbia.edu/">https://zero123.cs.columbia.edu/</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://huggingface.co/spaces/cvlab/zero123-live">https://huggingface.co/spaces/cvlab/zero123-live</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Zero-1-to-3 paper was released on March 20, 2023. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This method generates new views with changed viewpoints/camera angles based on a single RGBA image. It is based on the Conditional Diffusion Model, fine-tuned on the synthetic dataset to learn controls of the relative camera viewpoints. This is possible due to the geometric priors that large-scale diffusion models learn about natural images. Despite being trained on synthetic data, Zero-1-to-3 retains strong zero-shot generalization ability to out-of-distribution datasets and in-the-wild images.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Stable Zero123</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://stability.ai/news/stable-zero123-3d-generation">https://stability.ai/news/stable-zero123-3d-generation</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On December 13, 2023, Stability AI introduced the Stable Zero123 model, an advancement in the realm of object view synthesis. This innovative model distinguishes itself by delivering remarkably enhanced image quality compared to its forerunners, the Zero1-to-3 and Zero123-XL. The leap in performance is a direct result of improved training datasets (based on <a href="https://objaverse.allenai.org/">Objaverse</a>) and elevation conditioning techniques. The model can be used for non-commercial and research use, but a commercial version is also available.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) - Generating 3D objects from views</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The implementation of 3D object generation available in <a href="https://github.com/threestudio-project/threestudio">threestudio</a> was created by creators of the network. It uses <a href="https://dreamfusion3d.github.io/">Score Distillation Sampling (SDS)</a> to optimize a neural field (NeRF). Generating a mesh and texture from this NeRF can also be done, as a separate step. Stability AI team proposes using an SDXL network for the generation of a single image from text and then using the result as input for this method, as a way to accomplish Text-to-3D pipeline.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) is a way to generate samples from a diffusion model by optimizing a loss function.With SDS it is possible, to optimize samples in an arbitrary parameter space, such as a 3D space, as long as we can map back to images differentiable. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Using this loss in a DeepDream-like procedure,<br>we optimize a randomly-initialized 3D model (a Neural Radiance Field, or NeRF)<br>via gradient descent such that its 2D renderings from random angles achieve a low<br>loss.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In the original DreamFusion paper, released in September 2022, the randomly initialized 3D model, represented with NeRF, is optimized via gradient descent such that its 2D renderings from random angles achieve a low loss. The approach used a pre-trained and unmodified <a href="https://imagen.research.google/">Imagen</a> Diffusion 2D 64x64 pixels image generation model, so it did not require any training on 3D object datasets. Citing the authors:<br></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:quote -->
<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Our approach requires no 3D training data and no modifications to the image diffusion model,<br>demonstrating the effectiveness of pretrained image diffusion models as priors.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --><cite>DREAMFUSION: TEXT-TO-3D USING 2D DIFFUSION arXiv:2209.14988v1</cite></blockquote>
<!-- /wp:quote -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If I understood everything correctly, those are the steps of the generation process:<br>- The process starts with a randomly initialized NeRF field with albedo color and density data.<br>- An image is generated from NeRF and combined with some random noise. The NeRF rendering happens by casting a ray for each pixel from the camera's center of projection through the e pixel's location in the image plane and out into the field. For multiple points sampled along the ray, values for albedo and density (opacity) are sampled. <br>- This noise image is used as an input for the diffusion model conditioned on the text prompt.<br>- This model is used to generate a prediction of injected noise (diffusion models usually try to predict noise, so the image can be de-noised).<br>- This result is de-noised again, by subtracting a noise added earlier to the result of NeRF generation.<br>- This image can be interpreted as changes, that the diffusion model made to the rendering to make it match the prompt better. It is back-propagated through the NeRF and used to modify weights of the NeRF.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
